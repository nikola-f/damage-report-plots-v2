How Damage Report Plots works

Turn your Ingress "Damage Report" emails into a map of attacked portals — entirely in your browser.

When your portals are attacked, Niantic's Ingress sends you a "Damage Report" email listing which portals were hit, where they are, and when. Over months these pile up in Gmail. Damage Report Plots reads those emails, pulls out every portal and its coordinates, records them in a Google Sheet, and hands you a list you can paste into the IITC map plugin to see your damage history as a heatmap.

Step by step

1 Sign in with Google

You sign in with your Google account. The app uses Google Identity Services (the browser token model, with PKCE and no client secret), so there is no password to give the app and no long-lived credential: it receives a temporary access token that lasts about an hour and is never refreshed silently. You grant four scopes:

  • gmail.readonly — to read your damage report emails.
  • drive.file — access limited to the one spreadsheet the app creates; it can't see your other Drive files.
  • email and profile — to show who is signed in.

2 Scan your Gmail

The app searches Gmail for "Ingress Damage Report" messages from Niantic's sending addresses. It walks your history in 30-day windows, fetching message bodies in batches, so even years of reports come through without overloading the Gmail API. Each run resumes from where the last one stopped, so re-syncing only picks up what's new.

3 Parse the reports

Each email's HTML body is parsed in your browser to extract every attacked portal: its name, latitude/longitude, and the time of the report. Duplicate sightings of the same portal are merged and counted, so you get one row per portal with a hit count and the latest time it was attacked.

4 Write to your Google Sheet

The results are written to a Google Sheet that the app creates in your own Google Drive. The app tags this sheet with a private marker so it can find the same spreadsheet again on any device you sign in from — you keep a single, growing record instead of a new file each time. The sheet is yours: you can open, edit, or delete it whenever you like.

5 Copy & plot in IITC

Click "Copy plots JSON" and the app copies the portal list to your clipboard in the format the companion IITC plugin expects. Paste it into the plugin's dialog on the Ingress Intel map and your attacked portals render as a heatmap — brighter where you've been hit most.

Your data stays in your browser

Damage Report Plots has no backend server. Your Gmail messages, the access token, and everything derived from them are processed entirely on your device and are never sent to, stored on, or logged by any server we run. The only place your extracted data lands is the Google Sheet in your own Drive.

What you need

  • A Google account that receives Ingress "Damage Report" emails.
  • For the map step, the IITC browser plugin plus the Damage Report Plots heatmap userscript.
